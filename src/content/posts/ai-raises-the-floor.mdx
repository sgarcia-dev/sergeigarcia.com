Two things happened this autumn that tell the same story about AI.

The first is a music video about "P(doom)" that blew up in late September. It was made with Claude Opus 5.5, and it looks like the work of a professional motion designer: big, fast type, every word landing on the beat. Press play, it is worth the two and a half minutes.

<YouTubeEmbed id="5EoO5413dBY" title="P(doom) music video by @_mexicat" thumb={ytThumb} />

On [r/singularity](https://www.reddit.com/r/singularity/comments/1wr4bw8/at_least_the_ai_takeover_will_come_with_sick_music/) it passed 700 upvotes and 260 comments. The mood: this used to take a small studio, and now one person with an AI can do it. Some people went further and read it as one prompt, with the AI doing the rest.

The second is the site you are reading, which I built with AI agents over a couple of days.

Both look like "the AI did it." Both are really proof of the opposite: AI raises the floor, but human direction and feedback still set the ceiling.

## Exhibit A: the P(doom) video

[P(doom)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P(doom)) is short for "probability of doom": your own guess at how likely it is that AI ends badly for humanity. "What's your p(doom)?" has become an in-joke in AI circles.

Start with the song. Its lyrics were written by several people over a long time. [osmarks](https://docs.osmarks.net/hypha/p(doom)_song_objectively_correct_interpretation) wrote most of them in 2024, building on an opening verse and chorus by MusicPerson, with lines suggested by an online community. The music was generated with Suno. So the words, the jokes and the point of view came from people.

Then the video. Its author wrote a thread to answer the "one prompt" idea. These are the parts that matter:

The repo's [revision log](https://github.com/mexicat/pdoom-video) tells the same story: a realistic eye removed because "the client found it uncanny," and timings set by hand where the automatic lyric sync failed.

Look at what actually made it good. Not the prompt. The constraints and the notes: a tight palette, rules for the type, "don't illustrate every line," and tens of rounds of "not like that, try this." That is a director at work, with a very fast crew.

## Exhibit B: how my site was built

This is not the fastest website ever built, and that is not the point. The point is where the decisions came from. Three moments:

**1. The research.** Before any code, I had agents run a research spike: about twenty parallel runs across Claude, Codex and Gemini, comparing frameworks, hosting, design trends and SEO. They came back with a safe pick, a quiet editorial look. I chose something louder: yellow, cobalt and a big serif headline. The first version was live about 90 minutes after the research started.

  caption="AI widens your options. The final call stays yours."

**2. The extras.** Later I described the motion I wanted: comic-book halftone dots, a letter-by-letter text reveal I had seen on Meta's Muse Wonder page, a header that shrinks as you scroll. I asked Claude to push back and to pitch more ideas. It came back with a list: highlighter marks, sticker-style hovers, a scrolling ticker, a dot effect that follows the cursor and more. I kept five and cut the rest.

**3. The tuning.** Every idea went live on a throwaway copy of the site, and I judged it on the real pages. My notes were blunt. The first dots were darker than the yellow and made the text harder to read. A later round was "way too much... basically unusable." My favorite dots looked blurry: "it hurts my eyes." A flashy reveal on the big headings "screams too much," so a quiet, terminal-style one on the small text shipped instead. Five rounds for the backgrounds alone, in one afternoon.

## The point

AI is an extension of your skills, not a replacement for them. It made me faster at everything I already knew how to judge: design, code, writing. It did not know what I wanted until I told it, and it did not know it was wrong until I said so.

So stay in control:

1. **Ask for options.** Three versions teach you more than one. You find out what you want by seeing what you don't.
2. **Judge on the real thing.** A mockup lies. Try each option in the actual product, on real content.
3. **Set constraints early.** The video and this site both got better because of what they were not allowed to do.
4. **Give specific notes, and say why.** "The dots are too dark" is fine. "The dots are too dark, and I can't read the text on top" is better. The reason lets the agent fix the cause, not just the symptom.

The tools raise the floor for everyone. The ceiling is still yours to set.
